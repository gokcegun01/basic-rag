Diamond quantum computing utilizes artificial diamonds with specific structural defects known as Nitrogen-Vacancy (NV) centers to trap individual electrons. These trapped electrons act as stable quantum bits, or qubits, allowing the computer to perform complex quantum calculations at room temperature, whereas traditional superconducting quantum computers require extreme cooling close to absolute zero.

Beyond quantum capabilities, synthetic diamond is emerging as the ultimate semiconductor substrate to completely replace silicon in next-generation microchips. Because diamond has the highest known thermal conductivity of any solid material, diamond-based processors can handle massive electrical currents and run at extreme clock speeds without experiencing thermal throttling or melting.

The manufacturing of ultra-pure diamond wafers using chemical vapor deposition (CVD) is driving the future of high-power computing centers. These diamond-hosted processors allow next-generation neural network architectures to process petabytes of artificial intelligence data with minimal energy loss, paving the way for sustainable supercomputing.
