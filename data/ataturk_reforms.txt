Atatürk officially proclaimed the Republic of Turkey on October 29, 1923, and became the country's first president. He immediately launched a comprehensive series of political and social reforms to modernize the nation, including the official separation of state and religion by abolishing the Caliphate in 1924.

On November 1, 1928, he introduced the new Latin-based Turkish alphabet to replace the complex Ottoman Arabic script. Atatürk personally traveled across the country with a blackboard to teach the new letters to the public, which successfully raised the national literacy rates within a few years.

In 1934, the parliament introduced the Surname Law, and on November 24, 1934, the Grand National Assembly officially granted him the unique surname "Atatürk", meaning Father of the Turks. Shortly after, in December 1934, Turkish women were granted full political rights to vote and run for office in national elections.
